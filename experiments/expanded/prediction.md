**Experiment 2: expanded corpus.** `CORPUS = "classroom"` plus my 1,200 teaching passages in `corpus/extension/` (300 each for grammar, opposites, negation and spatial relations), with the same 3,000 steps and learning rate 0.001. Only the data changes.

- **Why these categories.** In experiment 1 all 24 extension cases were unscorable, because words like *not*, *opposite* and *below* never appeared in training. These four categories can be taught with ordinary words and short, varied sentences, without the test stories or their names. Reference, sequence, everyday knowledge and categories/analogies are left untaught as controls. If those stay flat, any gain in the four targets comes from the new teaching material rather than from training in general.
- **Coverage.** The vocabulary should grow from 136 to about 400 types, with no UNK tokens in training. 11 of the 12 targeted cases should become scorable. The exception is the negation case about *ava*, because I deliberately used no eval names. The 12 control cases should stay unscorable.
- **Starter cases.** These should stay at or near 16/16 and 4/8. The new vocabulary changes the random initialization, and the starter sentences now make up about 79% of the data instead of 100%, so small changes are possible.
- **Targeted cases.** I expect the "copy the corrected word" pattern (negation, `bag contains the …`) and the direction inverses (above→below, left→right) to be learnable, because the teaching stories use exactly that structure with other objects. Agreement (`one bird` → *is*, `the dogs` → *are*) should work if the model links nouns ending in -s with *are*. I'm least confident about `yesterday she` → *walked*, and about the opposites. The tested opposite pairs never appear in the `the opposite of … is` frame, so the model has to combine two different sentence types.
- **Loss.** Loss again starts near ln(vocabulary) ≈ 6.0 and falls. It is not directly comparable with experiment 1, because the vocabulary and the passages are different.
